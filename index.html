Do Urban Students Outperform Rural Students in Standardized Mathematics Tests? A Hypothesis Testing Approach

Introduction

Educational inequality between urban and rural areas is a persistent concern in Colombia. According to the Fundación Empresarios por la Educación (2024), students in rural areas scored 26.1 points less than urban students on the Saber 11 national exam in 2024, a gap that has remained consistent across recent years. At the international level, the OECD (2023) documents that access to education and learning outcomes are systematically worse in rural and disadvantaged areas across member and partner countries. This report applies hypothesis testing to evaluate whether the observed urban–rural score difference in Colombia is statistically significant or whether it could be explained by sampling variability.

Understanding whether this gap is statistically significant is crucial for evidence-based educational policy decisions (Suárez & García, 2024). The interactive hypothesis testing tool developed in C8 and C9 of this project was used to perform all calculations.

Research Question

Is the mean mathematics score of urban students significantly higher than the mean mathematics score of rural students, at a significance level of α = 0.05?

Methodology

A one-sample z-test for means was conducted using the benchmark mean established from rural student national averages. The test direction is right-tailed, since the hypothesis specifically evaluates whether urban scores are higher than the rural benchmark.

H₀: μ_urban = μ_rural    (μ₀ = 45.0 points)
H₁: μ_urban > μ_rural    (right-tailed)
α = 0.05

Data were drawn from the example dataset provided (G11_T3_L4_C8C9_example_mean_data.csv). The urban group served as the test group and the rural group as the benchmark. The tool automatically computed summary statistics and the z-statistic.

Results

The following results were obtained using the C8/C9 interactive webpage:

StatisticValue
Benchmark mean (rural, μ₀)45.0
Sample mean (urban, x̄)52.3
Standard deviation (σ)12.8
Sample size (n)60
Test statistic (z)4.42
P-value< 0.0001
Critical value (z*)1.645
Significance level (α)0.05
DecisionReject H₀

Since the p-value (< 0.0001) is less than α (0.05) and the test statistic z = 4.42 exceeds the critical value z* = 1.645, we reject the null hypothesis.

Discussion

The statistical evidence strongly supports the alternative hypothesis: urban students score significantly higher than rural students on standardized mathematics tests in this sample. This finding is consistent with official Colombian data. The Fundación Empresarios por la Educación (2024) reports that the urban–rural gap in the Saber 11 exam increased by one point between 2023 and 2024, reaching 26.1 points in the global score.

At the international level, the OECD (2023) documents that educational outcomes are systematically shaped by geographic location, with rural students facing disadvantages in access to qualified teachers, infrastructure, and learning materials. In the Colombian context, Suárez and García (2024) found that socioeconomic factors including internet access, parental education, and geographic location are strongly correlated with mathematics performance in the Saber 11, reinforcing that the urban–rural gap has structural roots that require targeted policy responses.

Statistical Errors in This Context

Type I Error (α = 0.05) Concluding that urban students score higher when in reality there is no true difference. This could lead to misplaced policy interventions that ignore other sources of inequality.
Type II Error (β) Failing to detect a real difference between urban and rural students. This would cause policymakers to overlook a genuine problem and delay necessary educational reforms.

Conclusion

At a significance level of α = 0.05, the hypothesis test provides strong statistical evidence that urban students score significantly higher than rural students on standardized mathematics tests (z = 4.42, p < 0.0001). The null hypothesis is rejected. These findings are consistent with official Colombian data showing a persistent urban–rural gap of over 26 points on the Saber 11 (Fundación Empresarios por la Educación, 2024), and underscore the need for equity-focused educational policies that address structural disparities between urban and rural schools (OECD, 2023; Suárez & García, 2024).
